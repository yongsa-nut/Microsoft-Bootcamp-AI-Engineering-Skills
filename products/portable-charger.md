The Contoso PowerVault 20K is a high-capacity 20,000mAh portable charger designed for travelers and power users. It delivers 65W USB-C Power Delivery output alongside an 18W USB-A port, enabling fast charging for laptops, phones, and tablets from a single compact unit. - **High-Capacity Battery**: 20,000mAh cell powers a typical laptop once and a smartphone up to four times.
- **USB-C PD 65W Output**: Fast-charges most USB-C laptops, tablets, and phones at full rated speed.
- **USB-A 18W Output**: Simultaneously charges a second device via Quick Charge-compatible USB-A port.
- **Pass-Through Charging**: Recharge the PowerVault while it charges connected devices — no waiting required.
- **LED Battery Indicator**: Four-LED indicator shows remaining capacity at a glance.
- **Airline Safe**: 72Wh rating complies with IATA carry-on battery regulations for commercial flights.
- **Compact Build**: At 12.5 oz, it fits in most jacket pockets and laptop bags without bulk.

## Known Issues & Limitations

- Simultaneous use of both ports may reduce total output; USB-C drops to 45W when USB-A is active.
- Pass-through charging is slower than wall-only charging; not recommended as a primary desk charger.
- Does not include a wall adapter in the box — USB-C PD adapter sold separately.
- Full recharge of the PowerVault itself takes approximately 2.5 hours with a 45W input adapter.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I charge my laptop and phone at the same time?
A: Yes. When both ports are active simultaneously, USB-C output adjusts to approximately 45W and USB-A delivers up to 18W. Most ultrabooks and phones will still charge at acceptable speeds, though USB-C-only mode at 65W will always be faster for laptops.

Q: Is it allowed on airplanes?
A: Yes, the PowerVault 20K is rated at 72Wh, which is below the 100Wh IATA limit for carry-on batteries. It is not permitted in checked luggage per airline lithium battery policy.

Q: How many times can it charge a smartphone?
A: Approximately 3–4 full charges for a typical 4,500mAh smartphone battery, accounting for conversion efficiency losses of roughly 10–15%.
